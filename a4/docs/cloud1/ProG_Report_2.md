My assessment: **the current result is not evidence that “MABs do not work” for zkVM fuzzing. It is evidence that this MAB is learning over the wrong action abstraction, with a reward that is anti-correlated with the local-constraint discovery objective.** The zoned strategy won because it encoded two structural priors the bandit did not have: kind fairness and explicit boundary-cycle sampling.

## 1. What the IV.POS.5 result really says

The headline result is decisive for this campaign: zoned reached **43.4/46** local contexts on average, bandit reached **37.4/46**, and uniform reached **35.0/46**. Zoned at mutation 1500 already matched bandit’s final coverage at mutation 6000, and the paired test for bandit vs zoned was significant at about p≈0.015. 

The important nuance is that the three strategies are **not comparable as clean variants of one design**. “Uniform” is not uniform over mutation kinds or trace steps; it is uniform over a 967-arm `(kind, bucket)` universe with `b_count=128`. “Zoned” is uniform over kinds and then uses a 5/90/5 init/core/final step prior. “Bandit-16” uses a 128-arm `(kind, bucket)` UCB universe with `b_count=16`, plus a nested step UCB. 

So the correct comparison is not “MAB vs uniform vs zoned.” It is:

**arm-uniform over a large bucket universe** vs **hand-coded structural prior** vs **UCB over coarse time buckets with a misaligned reward**.

That matters because zoned did not win by being statistically clever. It won because it assigned nontrivial mass to action regions the bandit almost never sampled.

## 2. The dominant failure mode: exact boundary structure hidden inside a bucket

The strongest evidence is §17.16. Four of the constraints responsible for the gap require the conjunction:

```text
mutation_kind = INSTR_TYPE_MOD
step = 0
```

For `MemLoadInput`, `MemStoreInput`, `MulInput`, and `DivInput`, the observed hit set across all runs was exactly `step={0}` and `kind={INSTR_TYPE_MOD}`. `ControlUserECALL@inst_control.zir:77` is separate: it is a `MEM_VAL_MOD` kind-allocation issue, not a step-0 issue. 

The budget allocation makes the result almost deterministic. Across five 6000-mutation seeds, zoned made **184** `INSTR_TYPE_MOD@step=0` attempts. Uniform made **1**. Bandit made **2**. That is the local-coverage gap in one table. 

This is a representational failure, not just a reward failure. A bucketed MAB cannot learn that `step=0` is special if `step=0` is buried inside a bucket and almost never sampled. The nested step UCB also did not help: within the favorite kind, bucket pulls were nearly uniform, and the report concludes that the nested step UCB was essentially doing uniform random selection. 

For RISC Zero, this is exactly the kind of pathology one should expect. The zkVM execution trace is a row-oriented object generated by the executor and checked by the prover; rows correspond to cycles, and validity is about whether the trace faithfully represents RISC-V execution. ([RISC Zero][1]) The RISC Zero proof system also uses control/data/auxiliary trace columns for execution, memory validation, lookup/range-check arguments, and constraints that can tap columns across cycles. ([RISC Zero][2]) A raw contiguous step bucket is therefore a weak coordinate. Boundary cycles, ECALL/MRET transitions, opcode class, memory role, and lookup/permutation role are much better coordinates than “bucket 0, bucket 1, …”.

## 3. The second failure mode: the reward ranks the best discovery kind as the worst arm

The report’s discovery-rate table is more damning than the headline result. Across all strategies, `INSTR_TYPE_MOD` produced **24.92 unique discoveries per 1000 mutations**, while the bandit’s favorite kind, `INSTR_WORD_MOD_SUR`, produced only **3.64** per 1000. Yet the reward ranks `INSTR_WORD_MOD_SUR` highest and `INSTR_TYPE_MOD` lowest. 

The per-kind reward decomposition explains why. `INSTR_TYPE_MOD` has high positive signal `S`, but it also produces many local/global failures, so the multiplicative quality penalty kills it. In the bandit runs, `INSTR_TYPE_MOD` had reward **0.036**, while `INSTR_WORD_MOD_SUR` had reward **0.150**. The report explicitly observes that the reward function ranks `INSTR_TYPE_MOD` worst even though it is the best kind for discovery. 

The current reward is effectively:

```text
reward = novelty_or_rarity_score × Q_loc × Q_rep × Q_glob
```

where `Q_loc = exp(-d_loc / tau_d)` and `Q_glob = exp(-d_glob / tau_g)`. The diagnostic shows `T_new` is nearly dead, `U` is zero, `Q_rep` is almost inactive, and the positive score is dominated by `F_new` and `F_rare`, then heavily modulated by `Q_loc × Q_glob`.  

That design punishes exactly the mutations that expose rich constraint structure. A mutation that trips four distinct local contexts can be a valuable exploration event, but with `tau_d≈2`, it gets an enormous multiplicative penalty. The report’s H1 is therefore right: `Q_loc` is anti-discovery in this campaign. 

I would phrase the root cause as:

**The reward mistakes “few failures” for “high-quality exploration.” In this zkVM trace-mutation setting, many valuable mutations are high-failure-density because they perturb a semantically central trace field.**

## 4. The third failure mode: UCB on mean reward is a bad fit for sparse, high-variance novelty

`INSTR_TYPE_MOD` is a “lottery ticket” kind: lowest mean reward but highest maximum reward. The report’s reward variance table shows `INSTR_TYPE_MOD` had the lowest mean reward but the highest max reward, while UCB optimized the running mean and therefore under-explored it. 

This is a classic mismatch between a stationary mean-reward bandit and a non-stationary discovery process. Constraint novelty is front-loaded, sparse, and path-dependent. Once a context is discovered, the same mutation family may become less rewarding even if it remains the only path to undiscovered rare contexts. The time-series diagnostic says `INSTR_TYPE_MOD`’s reward dropped sharply, but it continued to make discoveries later; the reward became anti-predictive of future discovery. 

So changing `c_explore` from 0.25 to 1.0 may help marginally, but it does not fix the core issue. You need either a different objective, a different action representation, or both.

## 5. The old global-hook reward fix was necessary but not sufficient

The previous report’s diagnosis was directionally correct: the old architecture was treating global behavior too coarsely through a Z-like signal, while Hook 3 contained structured global information.  The proposed fix was to fold global contexts into the same novelty/rarity machinery as local contexts and redefine `U` as a true “local-pass/global-pass but rejected” near-acceptance signal. 

But the new campaign shows a deeper problem. Even with extended contexts, raw global address coverage is noisy. Bandit and uniform slightly beat zoned on unique `(family, address)` global contexts, but the report notes that this axis is dominated by memory addresses and may have low semantic interest. 

So I would not use raw global context count as a primary reward. It is too easy for the bandit to optimize “different memory byte touched” rather than “different constraint mechanism explored.”

Use **semantic global contexts**, not raw global contexts. For example:

```text
GLOBAL_MEMORY:
  family = memory
  address_region = user | kernel | image | stack | heap | invalid | unknown
  address_bucket = page or log2-range bucket
  txn_role = read | write | ifetch | register | prev_word | prev_cycle
  cycle_phase = normal | ecall | mret | halt | boundary

GLOBAL_LOOKUP:
  family = u8 | u16 | cycle
  lookup_index_bucket
  producer_kind
  opcode_class
```

Raw address novelty can remain a secondary diagnostic, but it should not dominate the reward.

## 6. Are “unique constraints touched” and “number of constraints failed” the right signals?

**Unique local constraint contexts are a good primary endpoint. Total number of failed constraints is not a good reward signal.**

The local context metric is meaningful because it corresponds to distinct named constraint sites and major/minor contexts in the RISC Zero execution circuit. Your current campaign’s 46-context universe is a reachable subset of `(rv32im circuit × this guest × these mutation kinds × this instrumentation)`, not the full zkVM constraint universe. 

But `d_loc`, `d_glob`, and `n_fail` should be treated as **features**, not blunt multiplicative penalties. High `d_loc` can mean “garbage cascade,” but it can also mean “central trace structure perturbed.” In your data, `INSTR_TYPE_MOD` is high-`d_loc` and high-discovery. Penalizing it mechanically was the wrong move.

Better reward signals:

1. **New local semantic context**

   ```text
   (constraint_loc, major, minor)
   ```

   This remains the strongest proxy for local circuit-space exploration.

2. **New constraint family or file**

   ```text
   inst_mem, inst_mul, inst_div, inst_control, mem, u32, one_hot, ...
   ```

   This prevents the bandit from overfitting to many line variants inside one family.

3. **New structural trace cell**

   ```text
   mutation_kind × semantic_zone × opcode_class × mode × txn_role
   ```

   This would have rewarded `INSTR_TYPE_MOD@step=0` before any local context appeared repeatedly.

4. **Compressed global context**, not raw address context.

5. **Crash/no-telemetry rate**, tracked separately.
   Crashes should reduce trust in a sub-arm, but not cause the whole mutation kind to be abandoned if that kind is also the highest discovery source. Your data show `PRE_EXEC_REG_MOD` and `INSTR_TYPE_MOD` have elevated crash rates, and the report reasonably warns that crash-aversion may be coupled with discovery-aversion. 

6. **Near-miss categories**
   Keep `U`, but do not weight it heavily until it fires. In this campaign, `U` fired zero times across 89,499 mutations, so it was not an active reward component. 

The key change: reward **marginal discovery**, not “few failures.”

## 7. What I would change architecturally

### A. Replace `(kind, contiguous step bucket)` with `(kind, semantic zone)`

Your current arm space is too geometric. RISC Zero constraints are not organized by equal-width time buckets; they are organized by execution phase, instruction type, memory consistency, lookup/range-check machinery, ECALL/kernel transitions, and boundary conditions. RISC Zero’s zkVM implements RV32IM plus ECALL-style extensions and a user/kernel split; memory is sequentially consistent and aligned-load/store constrained. ([RISC Zero][3]) That strongly argues for semantic arms.

Use an arm space like:

```text
mutation_kind × semantic_zone
```

where `semantic_zone` includes:

```text
step0
last_step
pre_ecall
post_ecall
pre_mret
post_mret
pre_halt
post_halt
core_arithmetic
core_memory_load
core_memory_store
core_branch
core_mul
core_div
core_sha
core_poseidon
core_other
```

For the current campaign, `INSTR_TYPE_MOD@step0` should be an explicit singleton arm with a guaranteed minimum pull count. Do not bury it inside bucket 0.

### B. Drop nested per-step UCB for now

The nested step UCB is not learning. It has too many step candidates per arm and too few pulls per candidate. The report’s own distribution shows it behaves almost uniformly within buckets. 

Replace it with a structural sampler:

```text
select kind/zone
then sample a valid step inside that zone
```

For singleton zones, this deterministically picks the singleton. For broad core zones, sample uniformly or by opcode/txn role.

### C. Use a constrained bandit, not a pure bandit

A pure reward-maximizing bandit will abandon low-mean/high-discovery arms. You need exploration floors.

A practical policy:

```text
For each epoch of E mutations:

  50-60% reserved coverage floor:
    - uniform over mutation kinds
    - explicit minimum pulls for boundary zones
    - explicit minimum pulls for rare semantic zones

  40-50% adaptive:
    - Thompson sampling or bootstrapped bandit over kind × semantic_zone
    - reward = marginal new semantic coverage
```

This lets you keep the MAB contribution while preventing it from deleting the very regions that make zoned strong.

### D. Prefer Thompson sampling or bootstrapped sampling over discounted UCB

For this problem, I would test Thompson sampling before more UCB tuning. The reward distribution is sparse and high-variance. `INSTR_TYPE_MOD` is empirically a low-mean/high-jackpot arm. UCB on mean reward under-explores that pattern; Thompson sampling or bootstrapped posterior sampling is more likely to keep uncertainty mass on such arms. 

Use a Bernoulli success target:

```text
success = 1 if mutation discovers at least one new local or compressed-global semantic context
success = 0 otherwise
```

Optionally add a count model:

```text
count_reward = log1p(number of new semantic contexts)
```

This is better than optimizing the current scalar reward.

## 8. Reward redesign

I would stop using `Q_loc × Q_glob` as a multiplicative gate. Keep crash penalties, but do not multiply novelty by `exp(-d_loc/tau_d)`.

A better first reward for IV.POS.7:

```text
L_new = number of new local contexts:
        (constraint_loc, major, minor)

F_new = number of new local families:
        constraint file / named family

G_new = number of new compressed global contexts

S_new = number of new structural cells:
        (kind, semantic_zone, opcode_class, txn_role, sub_strategy)

crash = 1 if witness generation crashes or telemetry is missing

repeat = number of already-seen local contexts hit again
```

Then:

```text
reward =
    1.00 * sat(L_new, tau=1)
  + 0.30 * sat(F_new, tau=1)
  + 0.25 * sat(G_new, tau=3)
  + 0.15 * sat(S_new, tau=2)
  - 0.50 * crash
  - 0.05 * sat(repeat, tau=5)
```

where:

```text
sat(x, tau) = 1 - exp(-x / tau)
```

For bandit selection, you can simplify further:

```text
bandit_success = 1 if L_new + G_new_compressed + S_new > 0 else 0
```

and keep the richer scalar reward only for analysis.

What I would **not** do first:

```text
reward = 1 if any new local context else 0
```

as the only reward. It is directionally aligned but becomes too sparse once the 46-context universe is mostly saturated. Use it as a Thompson/Bernoulli success signal, not the entire experimental telemetry.

## 9. What to do about the three current strategies being incomparable

Rename them and stop treating them as symmetric baselines.

Current names obscure the actual behavior:

```text
uniform  -> arm_uniform_b128
zoned    -> kind_uniform_zoned_step
bandit   -> ucb_kindbucket_b16
```

Then run matched ablations:

```text
A. kind_uniform + uniform_valid_step
B. kind_uniform + zoned_step
C. kind_uniform + singleton_boundary_step
D. arm_uniform_b16
E. arm_uniform_b128
F. kind_UCB + uniform_valid_step
G. kind_UCB + zoned_step
H. kind_TS  + zoned_step
I. constrained_TS + semantic_zone_step
```

This will isolate which component actually matters:

```text
kind allocation
step-zone prior
exact boundary singleton handling
reward function
bandit algorithm
bucket count
```

Right now, zoned bundles kind fairness and boundary prior; bandit bundles UCB, b16 buckets, nested step UCB, and different calibration. The current A/B result is informative, but it is not a clean causal experiment.

Also lock calibration. The report shows uniform/zoned used fixed default calibration while bandit used a 100-mutation pilot with variable `tau_new` and tighter `tau_d`.  That is an avoidable confound. For IV.POS.7, use one deterministic shared calibration profile across all strategies, or remove calibration from the reward variants entirely.

## 10. What I would run next

### First: do not run current bandit at N=20k as the main next experiment

The fine-grained coverage curve shows bandit plateauing around mutations 3000-4000 and gaining little in the last 2000 mutations. The report itself says N=20k is likely to gain only a few contexts unless the architecture changes. 

A single N=20k run of the current bandit can answer “does it eventually stumble into step 0 enough times,” but it is not the main scientific test. It will mostly test brute-force luck.

### Run IV.POS.7 as a 6000-mutation ablation suite

Use five paired seeds first. If feasible, ten is better, but five is enough to triage.

Primary variants:

```text
1. zoned_current
   Reference. Must stay in the suite.

2. kind_UCB + zoned_step + current_reward
   Tests whether copying zoned’s step prior fixes the mechanical gap.

3. kind_UCB + zoned_step + no_Qloc_reward
   Same as 2, but remove/flatten Q_loc and Q_glob.

4. kind_TS + zoned_step + discovery_reward
   Tests whether posterior sampling handles high-variance discovery better.

5. constrained_TS + semantic_zones + discovery_reward
   Main candidate architecture.
   Enforce kind and boundary floors; use TS for remaining budget.
```

I would skip a large grid over `c`, `gamma`, and weights until these structural tests are done. Hyperparameter search on the current representation is likely to optimize around the wrong abstraction.

### Primary metrics

Use more than final coverage:

```text
local_context_final
local_context_AUC
time_to_40
time_to_43
time_to_46
per-seed all-46 hit rate
compressed_global_context_final
crash_rate
no_effect_rate
allocation entropy by kind and semantic zone
```

Because zoned already hits the full 46-context union, a better adaptive selector should be judged by **time-to-saturation** and **coverage AUC**, not only final count.

### Success criterion

A candidate survives if it does at least one of these:

```text
- beats zoned on local_context_AUC,
- matches zoned final local coverage with lower variance,
- reaches 43+ contexts materially earlier than zoned,
- preserves local coverage while improving compressed-global coverage,
- discovers a new context under expanded guests/mutation kinds.
```

If no bandit variant beats zoned on the current 8-kind IV.POS.5 task, the honest conclusion is that the structural prior dominates adaptive reward learning for this narrow target.

## 11. Should you expand mutation kinds now?

Not before fixing the selector/reward. Expanding from 8 to 10+ kinds increases sparsity and gives the current reward more room to misrank arms. The report’s own taxonomy warns that adding `TXN_PREV_WORD_MOD` and `TXN_PREV_CYCLE_MOD` would expand the arm space and possibly worsen convergence if the reward pathology remains. 

The right order is:

```text
1. Fix action representation and reward on the current 8-kind universe.
2. Demonstrate the selector behaves sensibly against zoned.
3. Add TXN_PREV_WORD_MOD and TXN_PREV_CYCLE_MOD.
4. Re-run with semantic zones and constrained exploration.
5. Add new guest programs that exercise BigInt/Poseidon/Keccak/control paths.
```

The current 46-context universe is not the full zkVM space; it is the reachable subset for this guest, circuit slice, mutation set, and instrumentation. The report explicitly notes that broader guests, recursion, FRI/poly-commitment, paging/session metadata, and accelerator-targeting mutations would expand the reachable universe.

## 13. Publishability

IV.POS.5 is publishable as a **negative diagnostic result** or as evidence that **structured trace priors beat naive reward-driven MABs on this zkVM target**. It is not yet publishable as evidence that your MAB framework works, and it should not be framed as evidence that MABs are inherently unsuitable.

The strongest story is:

```text
Naive MAB underperforms because zkVM constraint exploration is highly structured.
Equal-width time buckets and scalar rarity rewards miss singleton boundary events
and misrank high-failure-density mutation kinds.
Adding circuit-aware action structure and discovery-aligned rewards is necessary.
```

That is a valuable result, but you need IV.POS.7 to show whether the corrected architecture recovers or exceeds zoned.

## 14. Concrete next steps

1. **Freeze the current result.** Do not tune the current bandit further as the main path.

2. **Rename the strategies in the notebook/report** to reflect what they actually sample:
   `arm_uniform_b128`, `kind_uniform_zoned_step`, `ucb_kindbucket_b16`.

3. **Implement a semantic-zone step sampler** with explicit singleton zones:
   `step0`, `last_step`, ECALL/MRET/halt adjacency, and core opcode/memory classes.

4. **Implement one constrained adaptive selector**:
   kind/zone floors plus Thompson sampling over the remaining budget.

5. **Replace the reward for bandit learning** with marginal semantic discovery:
   new local context, new local family, compressed global context, new structural cell. Remove multiplicative `Q_loc`; keep crash as a separate penalty.

6. **Run N=6000 paired-seed ablations first.**
   Only move to N=10k or N=20k after a variant can match or beat zoned’s AUC/final coverage at N=6000.

7. **Do not add new mutation kinds until after this ablation.**
   Add `TXN_PREV_WORD_MOD` and `TXN_PREV_CYCLE_MOD` next, but only once the selector is not obviously misranking the current 8 kinds.

The main design principle is simple: **make the bandit choose among semantically meaningful zkVM trace perturbation regions, not arbitrary time buckets, and reward marginal discovery rather than low failure cardinality.**
