According to documents from June 15–16, 2026, the main conclusion is: **V5 is a real architectural improvement, but it is not yet evidence that post-execution trace fuzzing broadly beats Arguzz at bug finding.** It shows that **semantic-zone constrained exploration is excellent on A4’s reachable witness-trace surface**; V6/Arguzz shows that A4’s current mutation catalog is still far too narrow. The next move should be **hybridization plus bug-isolation machinery**, not more V5-only tuning.

## 1. What your new results actually prove

V5 worked. It is not a marginal improvement. It reached **46.4** final legacy constraint locations on average versus V1’s **42.9**, hit ≥46 on **10/10 seeds**, reached time-to-43 in **1017 mutations** versus V1’s **4366**, and found four novel kernel/ECALL-region locations not reached by V1. V2’s collapse onto `INSTR_WORD_MOD_SUR` also validates the old diagnosis: unconstrained UCB still learns the wrong allocation even after cleaner controls. 

The mechanism is also clear. V5’s win is not primarily “Thompson sampling got smart.” Its schedule is mostly deterministic floor coverage: **5615 floor pulls**, **233 adaptive pulls**, **144 cold pulls**, and **8 singleton pulls** per seed. The brief explicitly says V5 beats V1 by mutation 500 when only about 13 adaptive pulls have occurred; the win is “cold + floor on the right arm space,” especially semantic-zone arms such as kernel/ECALL-adjacent regions. 

That means V5 is best understood as:

> **A circuit-aware constrained exploration scheduler with a small adaptive tail.**

That is still useful. But it means the “MAB” part is currently secondary. The load-bearing pieces are:

1. The **semantic arm space**: `(mutation_kind, semantic_zone)`.
2. The **floor schedule** preventing arm collapse.
3. The **explicit boundary/kernel/ECALL zones**.
4. The removal of the old reward pathology.

This is not a criticism. In zkVM fuzzing, a constrained bandit is probably the correct primitive. Pure exploitation is dangerous because the reward landscape is sparse, non-stationary, and saturated quickly.

## 2. What V6/Arguzz changes

V6’s raw numbers look much better: **108.0** mean `constraint_loc`s per seed versus V5’s **46.4**. But the report is right that raw counts are not a fair head-to-head. V6 runs **11 mutation kinds**, including **7 V6-only kinds**, and about **79%** of V6’s pulls land on kinds A4 cannot currently use. Those V6-only kinds surface **105 V6-exclusive normalized locations** outside A4’s current reachable union. 

On A4-native territory, V5 is much stronger: the 51-location A4-reachable union is covered **50/51** by V5, **46/51** by V1, and only **20/51** by V6. V6 also hits **0/4** of the V5 novel kernel/ECALL locations even with 60k mutations and a wider kind set. 

So the correct interpretation is:

> **V5 beats Arguzz on A4’s current witness-trace territory. Arguzz beats A4 on total reachable terrain because Arguzz has a much broader mutation surface.**

This is a very productive result. It tells you that V5’s scheduler is strong, but A4’s mutation catalog is now the ceiling.

The V6 report says this explicitly: A4’s 8 kinds reach a 51-location union across 360k A4 mutations, while V6 surfaces 125 normalized locations in 60k mutations, including 105 locations unreachable to A4. The report concludes the ceiling is **kind-set-limited, not budget-limited**. 

## 3. Arguzz’s actual advantage

Arguzz is not just “uniform random but during execution.” The paper describes Arguzz as a tool for zkVM soundness and completeness testing that combines metamorphic testing with fault injection, generating semantically equivalent program pairs and injecting faults to mimic malicious or buggy provers. It was evaluated on six zkVMs, including RISC Zero, Nexus, Jolt, SP1, OpenVM, and Pico, and found eleven bugs; one RISC Zero bug led to a $50k bounty. ([arXiv][1])

That matters because Arguzz’s strength is not just breadth of constraints. Its fault-injection model often produces **semantically propagated executions**. A during-execution mutation can perturb a register, memory cell, branch condition, or PC and then let the VM naturally propagate consequences through later instructions. Your brief says Arguzz has an interstep-fire rate around **55%**, compared with about **23%** for A4, which means Arguzz more often creates multi-cycle cascades through real execution semantics. A4, by contrast, mutates one witness cell directly and is more surgical. 

For soundness bugs, this distinction is central. A valid RISC Zero proof attests to a valid execution trace; RISC Zero’s docs describe the execution trace as rows of machine state generated by the executor and checked by the prover for faithful RISC-V execution. ([RISC Zero Dev Docs][2]) RISC Zero’s zkVM implements RV32IM, extends it with ECALL, uses a user/kernel split, and has sequentially consistent memory execution. ([RISC Zero Dev Docs][3])

A4’s single-cell post-execution mutation directly attacks the witness/prover layer, which is excellent for mapping constraints. But a single-cell mutation is usually internally inconsistent by construction. Arguzz’s during-execution perturbations can be more naturally coherent because the rest of the trace is produced by execution after the fault. That coherence is exactly what you need when searching for accepted invalid executions.

## 4. Your cascade concern is valid, but needs refinement

Your argument is mostly right:

> If a mutation hits an underconstrained relation but always also triggers unrelated failing constraints, the verifier rejects and the soundness bug stays masked.

That is a real problem. A post-execution mutation that causes 20 local failures is less directly useful for soundness-bug discovery than a mutation that produces one failure, zero global residue, or—best—an accepted invalid trace. V3 and V4’s higher local coverage/depth can therefore be misleading. The report says V3/V4 achieve higher per-loc v2 depth than V5 but do not reach the four kernel/ECALL contexts V5 unlocks; V5 trades some depth for new-region breadth. 

But the hole in the argument is this: cascades are not useless. They are useful in **survey mode**. A high-cascade mutation tells you which constraint families are coupled to which witness fields. That is valuable for building a dependency graph. The mistake would be to treat cascade-heavy coverage as direct evidence of bug proximity.

You should split the architecture into two modes:

### Mode A: survey / cartography

Goal: maximize semantic constraint coverage, discover new locs, discover new compressed-global contexts, and learn co-failure structure.

V5 is good here.

### Mode B: isolation / repair / bug search

Goal: take a discovered region and minimize or repair co-failures until the invalid witness is either:

```text
accepted,
or rejected by a very small, interpretable failure set,
or proven unpromising under the current mutation model.
```

You do not yet have enough of Mode B. This is the most important missing layer.

## 5. Constraint-space exploration is a good proxy, not the final objective

Constraint-space exploration is a useful early objective because a fuzzer cannot find bugs in constraint regions it never reaches. V5’s discovery of the four kernel/ECALL contexts is therefore valuable. The RISC Zero spec also makes kernel/ECALL regions especially interesting: ECALL transfers control from user code to kernel handling and then resumes user execution, creating boundary behavior that is naturally bug-prone. ([RISC Zero Dev Docs][3])

But constraint coverage is not the same as soundness-bug yield.

For soundness bugs, the endpoint is:

```text
invalid semantic execution + proof accepted
```

For overconstraint/completeness bugs, the endpoint is:

```text
valid semantic execution + proof rejected
```

A4’s current mutation model mostly generates invalid witnesses and checks whether constraints reject. That is soundness-oriented, but single-cell invalid witnesses almost always reject. So local coverage is a **survey metric**, not a **bug metric**.

You need a metric stack:

**Exploration metrics**

```text
local_context_final
local_context_AUC
time_to_threshold
local_coverage_v2 breadth and depth
compressed_global_contexts, semantically bucketed
kind × zone allocation entropy
new region / new family discovery
```

**Bug-proximity metrics**

```text
verifier_accepted_invalid_count
proof_generated && d_loc=0 && d_glob=0 && rejected
minimum d_loc per semantic target
minimum d_glob per semantic target
singleton-failure rate
co-failure graph degree
repairability score
near-acceptance frontier size
```

**Isolation metrics**

```text
For each target constraint/family:
  min observed co-failure set size
  number of runs where target appears alone
  number of runs where target disappears but semantic invalidity remains
  number of successful local repairs
```

**Fair Arguzz/A4 metrics**

```text
coverage on common territory
coverage on A4-only territory
coverage on Arguzz-only territory
wall-clock per normalized discovery
applied mutation count, not attempted count
accepted invalid proofs per wall-clock hour
unique bug mechanisms, not just unique locs
```

The V6 report already does the right thing by normalizing location strings and separating A4-reachable territory from V6-exclusive territory. 

## 6. What to do with V3 and V4

V3 and V4 are useful as diagnostic variants, not production variants.

They show that removing `Q_loc` / using discovery-oriented reward and TS can increase depth or local activity, but without semantic-zone arms and constrained floor allocation, they do not unlock the important new regions. V5 is the only variant that uses semantic zones as arms; V1–V4 are kind-only or zoned-step variants. 

Your concern that V3/V4 may be producing less useful cascade coverage is plausible. The right way to verify it is not by total failure count alone. Compute:

```text
mean d_loc
median d_loc
p95 d_loc
singleton-failure rate
min d_loc per discovered loc
co-failure graph density
unique locs discovered with d_loc <= 2
unique locs discovered with d_glob <= 1
repair success rate
```

If V3/V4 dominate on total v2 cells but lose on low-cofailure discoveries and repairability, then they are mostly survey-depth variants. Keep them as corpus generators, not bug hunters.

## 7. Floor dominance in V5: what it means

The fact that V5 is mostly floor pulls is not a bug. It means your prior was correct: **the arm space matters more than the posterior at N=6000**.

But for the long-term goal—beating Arguzz at bug discovery—you should not leave V5 frozen with 94% floor forever. A scheduler that never lets TS dominate is not really adaptive. More budget will not fix this because the brief says the floor fraction remains structurally dominant unless you lower `coverage_floor_fraction` or change epoch sizing. 

I would change V5 into a staged schedule:

```text
Stage 1: Survey
  N = 1500–2500
  high floor, e.g. 0.55
  objective: discover broad local/CGC/zone territory

Stage 2: Adaptive exploitation
  N = 2500–6000
  floor decays to 0.20–0.30
  TS or bootstrapped TS selects arms based on:
    recent marginal discovery
    low-cofailure discoveries
    repairability
    underexplored semantic zones

Stage 3: Isolation
  focused mutations around rare/new regions
  multi-cell repair/minimization
  objective: accepted invalid or near-acceptance
```

Do not simply sweep `coverage_floor_fraction` around 0.50–0.65. That may tell you whether 0.55 is robust, but it will not answer whether adaptation can beat a strong structured baseline. Add a **decaying floor** variant:

```text
floor_fraction(t) = max(0.20, 0.55 × exp(-local_coverage_seen / K))
```

or an epoch-based schedule:

```text
epochs 0–20: 0.55
epochs 21–40: 0.35
epochs 41+: 0.20
```

Then compare V5-static versus V5-decay.

## 8. Should you expand A4’s mutation catalog?

Yes. This is now the highest expected-value path.

Earlier, I recommended fixing the selector before expanding the catalog. You did that. V5 passed the survival test. The old warning no longer applies in the same way. The new result says the bottleneck is the catalog.

Start with two tracks.

### Track A: import high-yield Arguzz-style kinds into the V5 scheduler

The V6 report says seven V6-only kinds—such as `INSTR_WORD_MOD` and `PRE_EXEC_MEM_MOD`—drive most V6 discoveries, and suggests selectively adopting high-yield Arguzz kinds rather than replacing the V5 stack. 

Do this first because it directly addresses the 105-location V6-exclusive terrain.

Priority order:

```text
1. INSTR_WORD_MOD
2. PRE_EXEC_MEM_MOD
3. PRE_EXEC_PC_MOD
4. BR_NEG_COND
5. POST_EXEC_REG_MOD
6. POST_EXEC_MEM_MOD
7. POST_EXEC_PC_MOD
```

But integrate them as V5 arms, not as a separate Arguzz uniform scheduler:

```text
arm = mutation_surface × mutation_kind × semantic_zone × opcode_class × pre/post
```

where `mutation_surface ∈ {A4_trace_cell, arguzz_exec_fault}`.

The scheduler must count only **applied** Arguzz mutations as pulls. Arguzz has applicability skew: some mutation kinds are no-ops when the instruction does not support them. The brief explicitly notes that Arguzz’s effective applied rate varies with opcode mix. 

### Track B: deepen pure A4 with untouched witness fields

The pure-A4 catalog is still valuable because it can reach proof/witness structures that during-execution fuzzing may not manipulate directly. The previous taxonomy lists high-priority unimplemented `TXN_PREV_WORD_MOD` and `TXN_PREV_CYCLE_MOD`, which target memory consistency and temporal ordering fields that A4 currently does not mutate. 

The more detailed brief says IV.POS.7’s 8 kinds touch a narrow part of the post-execution surface: mostly `txns[].word` plus `cycles[].major/minor`; many fields remain untouched. It recommends low-risk novel-field targets including `TXN_PREV_WORD_MOD`, `TXN_PREV_CYCLE_MOD`, and `CYCLE_MODE_MOD`, with medium-risk candidates such as `TXN_ADDR_MOD`, `TXN_CYCLE_PHASE_MOD`, `CYCLE_PC_MOD`, `CYCLE_STATE_MOD`, and `CYCLE_DIFF_COUNT_MOD`. 

My priority order for pure A4:

```text
1. TXN_PREV_WORD_MOD
2. TXN_PREV_CYCLE_MOD
3. CYCLE_MODE_MOD
4. TXN_CYCLE_PHASE_MOD
5. CYCLE_PC_MOD
6. CYCLE_STATE_MOD
7. TXN_ADDR_MOD
8. CYCLE_DIFF_COUNT_MOD
```

Delay high-risk structural/accelerator-internal mutations until you have better crash triage and repair logic.

## 9. Should you run Arguzz with your bandit logic?

Yes, but not as the main path.

A direct “Arguzz + V5 scheduler” experiment is scientifically useful because it isolates whether your feedback mechanism improves Arguzz’s uniform-over-applicable scheduler. But it is not the best endpoint architecture. The best endpoint is a **hybrid scheduler** that allocates between A4 trace-cell mutations and Arguzz-style execution faults.

Run three variants:

```text
V6-uniform:
  current Arguzz scheduler

V6-cTS:
  Arguzz kinds only, but selected by constrained TS over:
    kind × semantic_zone × opcode_class × pre/post

Hybrid-cTS:
  A4-V5 kinds + selected Arguzz kinds in one shared arm space
```

Primary comparison:

```text
V6-uniform vs V6-cTS
  tests whether your feedback improves Arguzz

V5 vs Hybrid-cTS
  tests whether importing Arguzz terrain improves A4 without losing V5's native advantage

V6-uniform vs Hybrid-cTS
  tests whether the final architecture can beat standard Arguzz
```

Use normalized location keys, applied-mutation counts, and wall-clock per discovery. Do not compare raw loc counts without territory decomposition.

## 10. The missing architecture: repair-guided witness fuzzing

To find soundness bugs, V5 needs a second engine after discovery.

Current A4:

```text
pick a witness cell
mutate it
observe failed constraints
reward discovery
```

Needed A4:

```text
pick a semantic target region
mutate one or more related witness fields
observe failed constraints
repair dependent fields to reduce unrelated failures
preserve semantic invalidity
retry until accepted or near-accepted
```

This is the bridge from coverage to bugs.

### Implement a co-failure graph

For every mutation, record:

```text
mutated field
semantic zone
opcode class
local failure set
global failure set
proof_generated
verifier_accepted
```

Build:

```text
field → constraints
constraint → co-failing constraints
field → repair candidates
```

Then define target clusters:

```text
kernel/ECALL cluster
memory consistency cluster
instruction decode cluster
PC/control-flow cluster
lookup/global residue cluster
accelerator cluster
```

### Add local repair templates

Examples:

```text
If txns[].word READ fails IsRead:
  try co-mutating prev_word

If txns[].prev_cycle breaks cycle residue:
  try updating linked prior/next transaction metadata

If cycles[].pc changes:
  try updating instruction-fetch address / decoded instruction relation

If cycles[].major/minor changes:
  try updating instruction word, opcode class, and dependent txns

If machine_mode flips:
  try aligning PC region user/kernel and ECALL/MRET control fields
```

The goal is not to “fix the trace” fully. The goal is to eliminate unrelated rejection reasons while preserving the semantic invalidity you care about.

### Add a minimization loop

After a mutation finds a rare/new/interesting region:

```text
1. Freeze the semantic invalidity.
2. Try reverting or repairing fields that cause unrelated failures.
3. Minimize d_loc and d_glob.
4. Prefer candidates with proof_generated=true.
5. Re-run prover/verifier.
```

This gives you bug-proximity rather than just coverage.

## 11. What to do about CGC / address bucketing

The Q1 concern is valid: `address_region × log2(address_bucket)` can still reward geometry rather than semantics. Your implementation has nine address regions and log2 buckets; the brief notes that user-space is still coarse because code/data/heap/stack/MMIO-like host ECALL buffers mostly collapse into `region=user`, leaving `address_bucket` as the discriminator. 

For reward, use **coarser CGC**:

```text
GLOBAL_MEMORY:
  family
  address_region
  txn_role
  cycle_phase
  opcode_class
  maybe page_class, not raw/log2 bucket

GLOBAL_LOOKUP:
  family
  lookup_kind
  producer_zone
  opcode_class
```

For analysis, report three CGC variants:

```text
CGC-region-only
CGC-log4
CGC-log2
```

But do not let log2 address buckets dominate TS success. The purpose is to reward different constraint mechanisms, not different bytes.

## 14. Direct answers to your smaller questions

### Are you using the right metrics?

Partly. `local_context_final`, AUC, v2 cells, CGC, and zone entropy are good exploration metrics. They are not sufficient bug metrics. Add isolation, near-acceptance, repairability, singleton-failure, and accepted-invalid metrics.

### Is constraint-space exploration the best way to find underconstraints?

It is a good **front-end**. It is not enough. Underconstraints are found when invalid witnesses are accepted. Constraint-space exploration tells you where to look; repair/minimization tells you whether the region contains an exploitable hole.

### What does floor-dominant V5 mean?

It means your semantic arm design is the main discovery engine. The adaptive posterior is not yet earning its keep. Keep the floor early, but add a decaying-floor or two-stage schedule to test whether learning helps after survey saturation.

### Is it worth running Arguzz with bandit logic?

Yes. Run `Arguzz-cTS` as an ablation, but prioritize `Hybrid-cTS` as the likely winning architecture.

## 16. Big question: can post-execution trace fuzzing beat Arguzz?

**Pure single-cell post-execution trace fuzzing probably will not broadly beat Arguzz on soundness bugs.** It can beat Arguzz on certain witness-layer subspaces, and V5 already beats V6 on A4-native territory, but Arguzz’s during-execution perturbations have a natural coherence advantage for accepted-invalid bug discovery.

**Structured post-execution witness fuzzing can beat Arguzz in specific regions Arguzz cannot reach well**, especially witness/proof-internal structures: memory permutation metadata, `prev_word`, `prev_cycle`, cycle-state fields, machine mode, lookup residues, accelerator backs, BigInt/Poseidon/SHA state, and eventually recursion/proof-system metadata.

The highest-probability path to beating standard Arguzz is therefore:

```text
V5 scheduler
+ Arguzz-style mutation kinds
+ A4 witness-internal mutation kinds
+ semantic constraint feedback
+ co-failure minimization / repair
+ normalized fair evaluation
```

That is no longer “pure A4 versus Arguzz.” It is a better fuzzer that uses A4’s strongest discovery mechanism and Arguzz’s strongest mutation surface.

The current V5 result is a strong foundation. The next proof point should not be “V5 gets more coverage on the same 8 kinds.” It should be:

> **Hybrid-cTS finds more normalized constraint mechanisms and more near-acceptance / accepted-invalid candidates per wall-clock hour than standard Arguzz.**
