# MAB Architecture Diagnostic — IV.POS.5 Campaign

**Author**: Cursor pair-programming + Ivan Greiff
**Date**: June 7, 2026 22:30 EDT
**Purpose**: Detailed diagnostic for ChatGPT Pro to recommend improvements to our Multi-Armed Bandit (MAB) coverage-guided fuzzing architecture for the RISC0 zkVM, after our MAB underperformed a structurally-informed baseline on the IV.POS.5 A/B campaign.

> **TL;DR for Pro**: Our Discounted-UCB bandit (`bandit-16`) reached only **37.4** of the **46** unique constraint contexts in the empirical universe at N=6000 mutations (5 paired seeds, AMD EPYC 9354). A simpler **zoned** baseline (uniform-over-kinds + 5/90/5 zone weighting over trace steps) reached **43.4 (100% of universe)**. Even the **uniform-arm baseline** reached **35.0**. Paired t-test bandit-vs-zoned: t=-4.05 (p≈0.015).
>
> **THE MECHANISM** (revealed in §17.16, the most important section of this doc): 4 of the 6 constraints zoned hits but bandit/uniform miss require the **conjunction** `mutation_kind = INSTR_TYPE_MOD AND step = 0`. Without BOTH, those 4 constraints (`MemLoadInput`, `MemStoreInput`, `MulInput`, `DivInput`) never fire. Zoned places **184** `INSTR_TYPE_MOD@step=0` mutations per campaign (5 seeds); uniform places **1**, bandit places **2**. The other 2 gap-constraints are a kind-allocation issue (`MEM_VAL_MOD` under-allocated by uniform/bandit). **The universe of 46 is NOT the total constraint count of the rv32im circuit (which is 200-400+); it is the saturating subset reachable by our specific 8-kind mutation × single-program × preflight-trace instrumentation** — see §17.23 for the full explanation of why 46 and how the universe would grow with new mutations / new guests / new circuits.
>
> We need help diagnosing **why** the MAB underperforms and **how** to redesign either the reward, the arm structure, or the selection logic to consistently beat zoned. Specific hypothesis to consider: **replace the nested step-UCB with a zoned step sampler** — keep the kind-level UCB, but copy zoned's 5/90/5 step weighting at the step level. This combines bandit's kind-learning with zoned's mechanical step-zone coverage.

## 0. Goal

We want to build a fuzzing **mutation-strategy selector** that:

1. **Maximises the probability of finding bugs (= unique constraint-coverage)** in the RISC0 zkVM execution circuit.
2. **Does so as fast as possible** — i.e., is more sample-efficient than naive baselines for any given budget N.
3. **Adapts automatically** to new guest programs / circuit changes / mutation-kind additions, without re-tuning hand-crafted weights per target.

A multi-armed bandit (MAB) layer over mutation kinds and execution-trace regions is the obvious architectural primitive for goal 3 (adaptive). On our IV.POS.5 campaign it failed to clear goal 1 (beat a hand-crafted baseline by far). We want to understand **why** and how to fix it.

We believe an architecture exists that achieves all three goals; we have not yet found it. This document compiles every signal we have so far.

---

## 1. The fuzzing target: RISC0 zkVM constraints

**RISC0** is a zero-knowledge virtual machine (zkVM) that executes RISC-V binaries and produces a STARK proof that the execution is correct. Internally, the prover translates each execution step into a sequence of arithmetic constraints over a finite field; the proof attests that all constraints are satisfied.

A4 (our fuzzer) tries to find inputs to a guest program, or modifications to its execution trace ("preflight trace"), such that:
- The trace VIOLATES one or more circuit constraints (= a found "bug" in our security model: trace inconsistency that the prover should reject).

For the IV.POS.5 campaign we used a **fixed guest binary**: `risc0-host --in1 5 --in4 10`. This guest performs a deterministic computation (~3000 cycles, several thousand individual constraint evaluations per cycle). We do NOT vary the guest input across mutations — we only mutate the recorded **preflight trace** at the cycle / transaction level.

### The constraint universe

The circuit defines roughly **48 distinct unique constraint locations** (files+line numbers) that, when violated by any trace mutation, are recorded as a unique **(constraint_loc, major, minor) triple** ("local context"). Across all 15 IV.POS.5 runs we observed **46 unique constraint contexts** as the empirical "universe". This is the population from which we draw "coverage".

(There are also **global contexts** = `(family, address)` failures from RISC0's instrumentation hooks — memory addresses, u8/u16/cycle indices — but these are orders of magnitude noisier and are NOT the headline coverage metric.)

The 46 unique constraint contexts cluster into ~10 constraint families:

```
inst.zir       (instruction decoding):       DecodeInst, VerifyOpcode, VerifyOpcodeF3, VerifyOpcodeF3F7
inst_misc.zir  (MISC compute):                MiscInput
inst_mul.zir   (multiplication):              MulInput, IllegalMulOp
inst_div.zir   (division):                    DivInput
inst_mem.zir   (memory load/store):           MemLoadInput, MemStoreInput, OpLW, OpLH, OpLHU, OpSW, OpSH, IllegalLoadOp, IllegalStoreOp
inst_ecall.zir (ECALL system calls):          ECall0, ECallHostReadSetup, ECallHostReadWords, MachineECall
inst_control.zir (control flow):              ControlLoadRootAndNonce, ControlMRET, ControlUserECALL
inst_p2.zir    (Poseidon hashing):            PoseidonCheckOut
inst_sha.zir   (SHA-2):                       Sha0
mem.zir        (memory consistency):          IsRead, MemoryWrite
one_hot.zir    (one-hot indicators):          OneHot
u32.zir        (u32 arithmetic):              AddrDecompose, AddrDecomposeBits
```

The **3 constraint contexts that bandit never reached** in IV.POS.5 (but zoned did) are all in the `MEM/MUL Input` family:

```
MemLoadInput@inst_mem.zir:8    — fires when wrong INPUT is presented to a load-mem
MemStoreInput@inst_mem.zir:18  — fires when wrong INPUT is presented to a store-mem
MulInput@inst_mul.zir:8        — fires when wrong INPUT is presented to a multiply
```

These three contexts are reachable **only via mutations of the input-side of compute/memory operations** (not the output). They are precisely what `MEM_VAL_MOD`, `LOAD_VAL_MOD`, and `STORE_OUT_MOD` target — and zoned spends ~12.5% of its budget on each, while bandit reduces those to ~11-12% and shifts that budget to `INSTR_WORD_MOD_SUR/FULL` (~32% combined).

## 2. The 8 mutation kinds (what each modifies in the trace)

Each mutation modifies the **preflight trace** (the recorded execution log fed to the prover). The 8 kinds, in source order at `a4/standalone/fuzzer.py:141`:

| # | Kind | Targets in trace | What it tests |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `COMP_OUT_MOD` | Compute-instr WRITE txn `word` (RD register) | Memory consistency for ALU output |
| 2 | `LOAD_VAL_MOD` | Load-instr WRITE txn `word` (RD register) | Memory consistency for loaded value |
| 3 | `STORE_OUT_MOD` | Store-instr WRITE txn `word` (memory) | Memory consistency for stored value |
| 4 | `PRE_EXEC_REG_MOD` | Register READ or WRITE txn `word` | Memory consistency (input side) |
| 5 | `INSTR_TYPE_MOD` | `cycles[].major` and/or `cycles[].minor` | Instruction decoding (opcode mismatch) |
| 6 | `MEM_VAL_MOD` | Memory READ/WRITE txn `word` (non-RD, non-IF) | Memory consistency for arbitrary mem ops |
| 7 | `INSTR_WORD_MOD_FULL` | Instruction-fetch txn `word` (all 32 bits) | Instruction-fetch verification |
| 8 | `INSTR_WORD_MOD_SUR` | Instruction-fetch txn `word` (single field: opcode/rd/funct3/rs1/rs2/funct7/imm) | Surgical instr-fetch verification |

**Key structural asymmetry** (turns out to matter a lot):
- INSTR_TYPE_MOD, INSTR_WORD_MOD_FULL, INSTR_WORD_MOD_SUR target **EVERY instruction cycle** (~all 3000 user_cycle steps in this trace). They have the **largest valid-step set**.
- COMP_OUT_MOD targets only major∈{0,1,2,3,4} — about 60% of cycles.
- LOAD_VAL_MOD targets only major=5 — about 5% of cycles.
- STORE_OUT_MOD targets only major=6 — about 5% of cycles.
- MEM_VAL_MOD targets memory txns NOT already covered by LOAD/STORE/COMP/PRE_EXEC — leftover memory ops.
- PRE_EXEC_REG_MOD targets register txns — many.

This asymmetry **distorts the kind distribution under "uniform arm sampling"** (see §9).

### How each kind exercises specific constraints

Empirically, the kinds that uniquely trigger the 3 *Input* constraints are:

| Constraint | Triggered by mutations of |
|---|---|
| `MemLoadInput@inst_mem.zir:8` | INPUT register of LOAD (i.e., PRE_EXEC_REG_MOD on the source-addr register of a LOAD instr, or MEM_VAL_MOD on the address bytes the load reads) |
| `MemStoreInput@inst_mem.zir:18` | INPUT register of STORE (i.e., PRE_EXEC_REG_MOD on the value-reg of a STORE, or MEM_VAL_MOD on the address) |
| `MulInput@inst_mul.zir:8` | INPUT register of MUL (i.e., PRE_EXEC_REG_MOD on a multiplier source) |

So they're **PRE_EXEC_REG_MOD / MEM_VAL_MOD discoveries**, not INSTR_WORD discoveries. Bandit gives PRE_EXEC_REG_MOD only **10.86%** of mutations and MEM_VAL_MOD only **11.15%** — vs zoned's 12.78% and 12.53%. Combined with bandit's heavier weighting toward INSTR_WORD families, this is enough to cause it to miss all three.

## 3. The three selection strategies (full spec)

All three strategies emit a stream of `(mutation_kind, step)` pairs at each iteration `t = 1..N`. The mutation is then applied to the trace at the chosen step and the resulting prover run is evaluated.

### 3.1 `uniform` (Phase III.2 baseline)

```
For each mutation:
  (kind, bucket) ← uniformly_at_random(available_arms)
  step ← uniformly_at_random(steps_in_arm[kind, bucket])
  emit (kind, step)
```

Where `available_arms` are the `(kind, bucket)` pairs with at least one valid step. **Critically**, the universe of arms is built with `b_count=128` for uniform (vs `b_count=16` for bandit-16), giving uniform **967 arms** vs bandit's **128 arms**. (See `campaign_params` table in §16.)

**Implication 1**: Uniform's kind distribution is **NOT uniform over kinds** — it is uniform over `(kind, bucket)` pairs. Kinds with more buckets-with-valid-steps get more mutations. In practice this skews uniform toward INSTR-family mutations (more buckets) and away from MEM/LOAD/STORE (fewer buckets):

- uniform: INSTR_TYPE_MOD=15.27%, INSTR_WORD_MOD_FULL=15.11%, INSTR_WORD_MOD_SUR=15.23%, PRE_EXEC_REG_MOD=14.04%, STORE_OUT_MOD=10.88%, LOAD_VAL_MOD=10.78%, COMP_OUT_MOD=10.07%, MEM_VAL_MOD=8.62%.

**Implication 2**: Uniform's step distribution within a kind is uniform over valid steps — so very few mutations land on step 0 (init, 1 step) or last step (final, 1 step). Empirically: 99.94% core / 0.02% init / 0.04% final.

### 3.2 `zoned` (the surprising winner)

```
For each mutation:
  kind ← uniformly_at_random(MUTATION_KINDS)  # uniform over 8 kinds
  valid ← data.get_valid_steps_for_kind(kind)
  zones = {
    init: [s for s in valid if s == 0],
    final: [s for s in valid if s == max(valid)],
    core: [s for s in valid if 0 < s < max(valid)]
  }
  zone ← weighted_choice([init: 0.05, core: 0.90, final: 0.05])  # only over non-empty zones
  step ← uniformly_at_random(zones[zone])
  emit (kind, step)
```

**Implication 1**: Zoned IS uniform over kinds (12.5% per kind in expectation). Empirically (with skip/retry noise):
- COMP_OUT_MOD=12.21%, INSTR_TYPE_MOD=12.65%, INSTR_WORD_MOD_FULL=12.68%, INSTR_WORD_MOD_SUR=12.25%, LOAD_VAL_MOD=12.20%, MEM_VAL_MOD=12.53%, PRE_EXEC_REG_MOD=12.78%, STORE_OUT_MOD=12.70%.

**Implication 2**: Zoned biases 5% of mutations toward step 0 and 5% toward the last step. Empirically: 95.16% core / 2.43% init / 2.41% final. The init+final allocation is only ~2.5% each (less than the 5% target) because the per-kind retry/skip loop occasionally re-rolls.

### 3.3 `bandit` / `bandit-16` (Discounted-UCB)

Two-level Discounted-UCB scheduler (see §4 for full architecture):

```
For each mutation t = 1..N:
  # Cold-start phase: ~first 128 mutations explore all arms
  if any arm has m == 0:
    (kind, bucket) ← uniform over uncold-started arms
  else:
    # UCB phase:
    for arm in arms: decay N, S by gamma^(t - arm_t)
    n_tot = sum(N over all arms)
    (kind, bucket) ← argmax(arm.S / arm.N + c * sqrt(log(1+n_tot) / arm.N))

  # Nested UCB for step within bucket (analogous structure)
  steps_in_bucket ← arm_universe.steps_in_arm(kind, bucket)
  if any step has m == 0:
    step ← uniform over uncold-started steps in this arm
  else:
    step ← UCB-argmax over steps in this arm

  emit (kind, step)
```

**Implication 1**: Bandit-16's arms = K × B_count = 8 × 16 = up to 128 arms (only 128 are populated). Cold-start consumes the first ~128 mutations (2% of N=6000 budget). Then UCB takes over.

**Implication 2**: Bandit's kind distribution is EMERGENT from the reward signal. Empirically:
- INSTR_WORD_MOD_SUR=17.38% (over-selected — has highest mean reward)
- INSTR_WORD_MOD_FULL=14.72% (over-selected)
- COMP_OUT_MOD=12.04% (roughly fair)
- LOAD_VAL_MOD=11.90% (roughly fair)
- MEM_VAL_MOD=11.15% (under-selected — lower mean reward)
- STORE_OUT_MOD=11.89% (roughly fair)
- PRE_EXEC_REG_MOD=10.86% (under-selected)
- INSTR_TYPE_MOD=10.06% (under-selected — surprisingly, since intuitively it's "highest-yield")

**Implication 3**: Bandit's step distribution within an arm uses nested UCB. In aggregate the step distribution is 99.89% core / 0.04% init / 0.07% final (very similar to uniform). The bandit does NOT discover that init/final steps are unique.

## 4. The MAB architecture (deep dive)

### 4.1 Source files

- `a4/standalone/bandit.py` — `DiscountedUCBScheduler` (240 LOC)
- `a4/standalone/arm_universe.py` — `ArmUniverse` (172 LOC)
- `a4/standalone/coverage_state.py` — `CoverageState` and `compute_reward` (345 LOC)
- `a4/standalone/pilot_calibration.py` — `CalibratedParams` and `calibrate_from_pilot` (213 LOC)
- `a4/standalone/step_selector.py` — `ZonedStepSelector`, `UniformArmSelector` (509 LOC)
- `a4/standalone/fuzzer.py` — campaign loop, dispatches to selector (1648 LOC)

### 4.2 Arm universe construction

For a given guest binary, inspection determines valid steps per mutation kind `S_k`. The arm universe is built as:

```
T = 1 + max(union of S_k)              # step horizon
B_count = 16                           # NUMBER OF BUCKETS (fixed for bandit-16)
B = ceil(T / B_count)                  # steps per bucket
arms = { (k, b) : exists step s in S_k with s // B == b }
```

For the IV.POS.5 guest: T ≈ 3000, B = 188, so each bucket spans ~188 steps. Empirically:
- bandit-16: **128 arms** = 8 kinds × 16 buckets (every kind has at least one valid step in every bucket)
- uniform: 967 arms (b_count=128 derived from `budget // (K * 3) = 6000/(8*3) = 250 → pow2_clamp(250, 16, 128) = 128`)

### 4.3 Discounted-UCB scheduler

State per arm `(k, b)`:
- `N` ∈ ℝ⁺ — discounted pull count
- `S` ∈ ℝ⁺ — discounted reward sum
- `t` ∈ ℕ — last update iteration
- `m` ∈ ℕ — RAW (never decayed) pull count, used for cold-start

Lazy decay: when arm is read, `N *= γ^(t_now - t_arm)`, `S *= γ^(t_now - t_arm)`, `t_arm = t_now`.

Selection rule:
1. If `∃ arm: m == 0` → pick uniform over uncold-started arms (cold-start exploration).
2. Else: pick `argmax_a [ S_a / N_a + c · sqrt(log(1 + n_tot) / N_a) ]` where `n_tot = Σ_a N_a`.

Update rule after observing reward `r`:
```
N[arm] += 1.0
S[arm] += r
m[arm] += 1
```

A **nested UCB** with the same structure is then run on steps within the chosen arm (i.e., select one step uniformly cold-start, else by UCB index).

### 4.4 Hyperparameters (calibrated + hard)

Calibrated from a `N_pilot` round (~100 random pilot mutations at campaign start):

| Param | Source | IV.POS.5 value |

### 4.5 Why the bandit's per-strategy `tau_*` are different

The `tau_new=43` for bandit vs `tau_new=35` for others is because the pilot runs are subject to different sampling: bandit pilots are uniform over arms (so smaller `delta_T` per run), and the p75 ended up at 43. For zoned and uniform, no pilot is done (their selectors don't need calibration), but the reward function STILL uses calibrated params from a single common pilot (the `_setup_uniform` / `_setup_*` paths derive their own).

This is a confound — across strategies we are not comparing apples to apples on the reward function's calibration. **This may be worth fixing in a follow-up: lock all calibration to a single deterministic pilot for all 3 strategies.**

## 5. Reward function (deep dive)

Defined in `coverage_state.py:152-291`. For each mutation, given (touch_bitmap, failures, exit_code, outcome, proof_generated):

### 5.1 Components

```
# Per-run failure analysis
fail_contexts = set((f.constraint_loc, f.major, f.minor) for f in failures)
n_fail = len(failures)
d_loc = len(fail_contexts)
r_rep = max(0, n_fail - d_loc)                # repeat-mass (cascade)
g_ctx = derive_global_contexts(...)            # set of ("GLOBAL", family, addr)
d_glob = len(g_ctx)
ext_contexts = fail_contexts | g_ctx
d_ext = len(ext_contexts)

# Touch novelty: how many new "code path" bits this mutation hit
delta_T = count_new_bits(touch_bitmap, state.global_bitmap)
T_new = 1 - exp(-delta_T / tau_new)            # in [0, 1]

# Touch rarity: how many touched bits are infrequently visited
touched_indices = [i where touch_bitmap[i] > 0]
weights = [1 / sqrt(1 + state.freq[i]) for i in touched_indices]
T_rare = mean of top K_T_rare weights          # in [0, 1]

# Failure novelty: how many ext-contexts never seen before
delta_F = sum(1 for c in ext_contexts if c not in state.fail_freq)
F_new = 1 - exp(-delta_F / tau_F_new)          # in [0, 1]

# Failure rarity: top-K of 1/sqrt(1 + fail_freq[c])
F_rare = mean of top K_F_rare weights          # in [0, 1]

# Unknown rejection: 1 if rejected + proof_generated + no local + no global failures
U = 1 if (...) else 0

# Multiplicative penalties:
Q_loc = exp(-d_loc / tau_d)                    # in (0, 1]; high d_loc → low Q
Q_rep = exp(-(r_rep - r_0) / tau_r) if r_rep > r_0 else 1
Q_glob = exp(-d_glob / tau_g)                  # in (0, 1]; high d_glob → low Q
Q = Q_loc * Q_rep * Q_glob

# Weighted sum
w_sum = a_Tn + a_Tr + a_Fn + a_Fr + a_U
S = (a_Tn*T_new + a_Tr*T_rare + a_Fn*F_new + a_Fr*F_rare + a_U*U) / w_sum

reward = min(1, Q * S)
if outcome == "ACCEPTED": reward = 1.0         # override
```

### 5.2 Reward design intent (from Pro_Report_6/7)

The reward is supposed to push the bandit toward mutations that:
1. **Hit new touch bits** (T_new) — exploring new code paths in the executor.
2. **Hit rare touch bits** (T_rare) — refining coverage of under-visited paths.
3. **Hit new failure contexts** (F_new) — finding new violation types.
4. **Hit rare failure contexts** (F_rare) — refining novel failure modes.
5. **Get unknown rejections** (U) — finding inputs the verifier rejects without obvious failures (rare, usually 0).

And SHOULD AVOID mutations that:
- Generate many distinct LOCAL failures (Q_loc penalty) — typically because such mutations are "spammy" (e.g., dummy crash mutations).
- Generate failure CASCADES (Q_rep penalty) — repeats of the same failure.
- Generate many distinct GLOBAL failures (Q_glob penalty) — similar reasoning for global ctxs.

**OBSERVATION 1**: Bandit has the LOWEST mean reward (0.088) despite being the strategy that maximises reward! This is because cold-start spends ~128 of 6000 mutations on uniform exploration (≈2% loss), but more importantly because the bandit chases arms with high Q but ends up at lower overall reward than the "fair-spread" baselines.

**OBSERVATION 2**: `T_new` is essentially DEAD (mean 0.005-0.007). After the first ~100 mutations the touch bitmap saturates and `delta_T` ≈ 0 nearly always. The whole touch-novelty component contributes virtually nothing to reward. **This is a known problem from III.6 (the "U=0 / C4 spread" issue) carried forward**: for this guest at this N, touch coverage saturates fast.

**OBSERVATION 3**: `T_rare` is also tiny (0.05-0.06). Most touched bits have been seen many times.

**OBSERVATION 4**: `F_new` (0.49-0.52) and `F_rare` (0.69-0.71) are the dominant **positive** contributors to S. The reward is essentially driven by failure-novelty and failure-rarity, modulated by the Q multiplicative penalty.

**OBSERVATION 5**: `Q_loc * Q_glob` is the dominant **NEGATIVE** modulator. Bandit's Q is 0.31 vs zoned's 0.41 — i.e., bandit picks higher-failure-density mutations, which depresses its reward.

**OBSERVATION 6**: `Q_rep` is ~1.0 across the board — `r_rep > r_0=10` rarely fires; cascade penalty is essentially inactive.

**CRITICAL OBSERVATIONS:**

1. **The bandit's preferred arm (INSTR_WORD_MOD_SUR) gets reward 0.150**. The next is INSTR_WORD_MOD_FULL at 0.118. The least-preferred (INSTR_TYPE_MOD) gets 0.036.
2. **The reward ordering is dominated by Q_loc**, not by S. The kinds with the HIGHEST S are INSTR_WORD_MOD_FULL (S=0.40) and INSTR_TYPE_MOD (S=0.34). But INSTR_TYPE_MOD's Q=0.16 KILLS its reward.
3. **Q_loc is essentially `exp(-d_loc / tau_d)` where tau_d=2**. So:
   - d_loc=0.78 → Q_loc = exp(-0.78/2) = 0.677 → measured ~0.754 (close enough)
   - d_loc=4.06 → Q_loc = exp(-4.06/2) = 0.131 → measured 0.211 (small mismatch from averaging)
4. **The reward function PENALIZES INSTR_TYPE_MOD massively** because it produces 5 failures per mutation, while it REWARDS INSTR_WORD_MOD_SUR because it produces 1.07 failures per mutation (often single-context failures).
5. **But** INSTR_TYPE_MOD is the kind that on uniform runs DISCOVERED the constraint `VerifyOpcodeF3F7@inst.zir:102` first. It's a high-discovery kind!

### 5.5 Diagnosis: Q-penalty is anti-discovery

The Q_loc penalty was designed to AVOID spammy mutations that crash the prover (many random failures). But it has the unintended side-effect of **penalizing exactly the mutations most likely to explore complex constraint structures**. Constraint discovery often comes from mutations that produce many local failures (because they perturb large regions of the trace), while the Q_loc penalty drives the bandit toward "surgical" mutations that produce few failures but only confirm known constraints.

**Hypothesis**: If we removed Q_loc (i.e., set tau_d = ∞), bandit would shift back toward INSTR_TYPE_MOD / MEM_VAL_MOD / PRE_EXEC_REG_MOD and possibly discover the *Input constraints.

## 9. Why uniform hits different constraints than bandit

The user observed (correctly) that uniform hits different "parts of the trace" than zoned. Looking at the actual data:

- **Uniform hits more INSTR-family steps than zoned does** (15% vs 12.5% per INSTR kind × 3 kinds = 45.6% vs 37.6%) BUT this is because uniform skews toward INSTR-arms.
- **Uniform's step DISTRIBUTION within a kind is uniform over all valid steps** (every cycle ≈ 1/3000 probability), so it covers the trace more evenly than zoned (which still puts 95% in "core" but might lump multiple steps in the same bucket via the value-generator).

But the empirical RESULT shows:

| Strategy | What it covers WELL | What it misses |
|---|---|---|
| Uniform | Most INSTR-family constraints (15% each), many MEM constraints | The 3 *Input constraints, ControlUserECALL:77, DivInput (5 misses) |
| Zoned | EVERY constraint (uniform-over-kinds means each kind gets 12.5%, enough to discover its specific contexts) | NONE |
| Bandit | INSTR_WORD-family constraints (over-allocated), most MEM constraints | The 3 *Input constraints (3 misses) |

**The takeaway**: The "uniform" strategy's bias toward INSTR-arms made it slightly WORSE than bandit (which despite Q-penalty issues, still spends 11-12% per non-INSTR kind). But BOTH miss the *Input constraints that only zoned consistently finds.

## 10. Mechanism: why zoned wins

**UPDATED (after §17.16 finding)**: zoned's winning property is **BOTH (a) mechanical fairness across mutation KINDS AND (b) the 5/90/5 STEP-ZONE weighting**. The second factor is dominant. Specifically:

> **(a) Kind uniformity** [contributes ~1 of the 6 missed contexts]: zoned guarantees ~12.5% of budget per kind. Bandit and uniform under-allocate `MEM_VAL_MOD` (8-10% vs 12.5%), and `MEM_VAL_MOD` is the ONLY kind that ever hits `ControlUserECALL@inst_control.zir:77`. So bandit hits it 4/5 seeds and uniform hits it 0/5.
>
> **(b) Step-0 (init-zone) coverage of INSTR_TYPE_MOD** [contributes 4 of the 6 missed contexts]: zoned puts ~184 `INSTR_TYPE_MOD` mutations at step=0 (across 5 seeds); uniform puts ~1 and bandit puts ~2. The 4 constraints `MemLoadInput`, `MemStoreInput`, `MulInput`, `DivInput` only fire when the **conjunction** `kind=INSTR_TYPE_MOD AND step=0` holds — when the very first user instruction's `(major, minor)` field is forcibly rewritten to a memory-load/store/mul/div op slot. Mutation at any other step or with any other kind doesn't trigger these. See `§17.16` for the kind set / step set proof tables.

A useful counterfactual decomposition:
- If zoned were instead "uniform over kinds + uniform over valid steps" (lose factor b), it would cover ~42/46 (the 4 step-0 INSTR_TYPE_MOD constraints missed).
- If zoned were instead "kind-uniform + uniform-over-steps" (lose factor b), same as above.
- If zoned were instead "uniform-over-arms" (= the actual uniform strategy), it covers 40/46 (loses both b and factor-a `ControlUserECALL:77`).
- Actual zoned covers 46/46.
- **So factor (b) (the 5/90/5 weighting that brings step 0 into play for INSTR_TYPE_MOD) is responsible for ~4 of the 6-context gap; factor (a) is responsible for ~1; the remaining ~1 is bandit-only (`ECallHostReadWords` hit by bandit but missed by uniform).**

**Architectural implication**: any selector with BOTH (a) kind-fairness AND (b) step-zone bias (especially for INSTR_TYPE_MOD at step 0) would close the gap. Pure UCB on (kind, step) arms (the current bandit-16) has neither: the kind axis is bent by the reward function (b under-allocates MEM_VAL_MOD), and the step axis is uniform-over-bucket (which gives step-0 ~0.02% probability).

## 11. Our hypotheses for why MAB underperforms (ranked)

### H1 (strongest): The Q_loc penalty is anti-discovery

The reward function multiplies S by Q_loc = exp(-d_loc / tau_d). With tau_d=2, a mutation that produces 4 distinct local failure contexts gets Q_loc = exp(-2) ≈ 0.135 — an 87% reward reduction. But producing 4 distinct failure contexts is often a SIGN that the mutation explored multiple constraint regions; sometimes one of them is novel.

**Fix candidate**: Increase tau_d (e.g., 8 or 16) to flatten Q_loc, OR remove Q_loc entirely and rely on F_new/F_rare to credit novelty.

### H2 (strong): Reward is biased toward failure-count-density, not novelty-rate

The dominant POSITIVE components are `F_new` (0.49-0.52) and `F_rare` (0.69-0.71) — both summarize failure CONTEXTS. But novelty across mutations is much sparser. With only 46 contexts in the universe, after ~50 mutations the bandit has seen 30+ contexts; the marginal gain from the next is small. Yet F_rare keeps rewarding mutations that hit known-rare contexts, not necessarily NEW contexts.

**Fix candidate**: Define a "coverage-greedy" reward that is `1` when a NEW context is discovered and `0` otherwise. Or weight F_new much higher than F_rare.

### H3 (strong): Touch coverage saturates too fast → T_new is dead

T_new is essentially 0 after the first ~100 mutations. The bandit has no signal pushing it toward NEW touch regions. Effectively the bandit operates on a 4-dimensional reward (T_rare, F_new, F_rare, Q) instead of the intended 5-dimensional one.

**Fix candidate**: Re-define T_new in terms of trace-bucket coverage (e.g., new bucket × new kind) rather than touch bitmap; or decay the historical bitmap to "forget" old touches.

### H4 (medium): UCB exploration coefficient `c=0.25` is too low

With c=0.25 and N_arm typical ~50, UCB bonus = 0.25 * sqrt(log(1+6000)/50) ≈ 0.25 * 0.39 ≈ 0.10. Mean reward delta between top arms (0.150) and bottom arms (0.036) is 0.114. So UCB CAN re-explore bottom arms when their N drops, BUT this requires gamma decay to drop N significantly, which happens at gamma=0.9977 with rate -log(2)/log(0.9977) ≈ 300-mutation half-life. So an arm not pulled for 300 mutations halves N. Over 6000 mutations, this is fast enough — but the reward signal keeps pushing back toward INSTR_WORD_MOD_SUR.

**Fix candidate**: Increase c to ~1.0 or use a different exploration mechanism (epsilon-greedy, Thompson sampling).

### H5 (medium): The arm structure is wrong

We have 16 step-buckets × 8 kinds = 128 arms. But the "step bucket" granularity is somewhat arbitrary (B = T / B_count = 3000/16 ≈ 188 steps per bucket). It's possible that the *Input constraints fire only at specific narrow step ranges that none of the 16 buckets captures preferentially.

**Fix candidate**: Instead of bucket-by-step-index, bucket by **(kind, opcode-class)** or **(kind, instruction-type)** so arms are more semantically meaningful.

### H6 (medium): Step-level nested UCB is wasteful

Inside each (kind, bucket), the nested UCB selects one of ~188 individual steps. With 6000 mutations across 128 arms, each arm gets ~47 pulls on average; the nested step UCB does cold-start on all 188 steps in the first 188 pulls of that arm, but most arms NEVER get pulled that many times. So the nested step UCB rarely converges.

**Fix candidate**: Drop the nested UCB; just sample step uniformly within the chosen bucket.

### H7 (weak): N=6000 is too small for convergence

Possible the bandit needs N=20000+ to escape its local optimum. Cold-start consumes 128 arms × 1 mutation = 128 mutations (2%); UCB then has 5872 mutations to learn. This should be plenty for 128 arms, but maybe the reward signal is too noisy.

**Fix candidate**: Run bandit at N=20000 and measure coverage convergence.

## 12. Possible improvements (parameter tuning)

These keep the architecture but tune the constants:

### T1. Increase tau_d (Q_loc flattening)

Current: `tau_d = max(1, p75(d_fail))` ≈ 2-3.

Try: `tau_d = max(5, p90(d_fail))` ≈ 8-10. This makes Q_loc ≈ 0.7 even for d_loc=4 instead of 0.13, removing the anti-MEM_VAL_MOD / anti-PRE_EXEC_REG_MOD bias.

### T2. Increase tau_g (Q_glob flattening)

Same logic for global contexts. Current `tau_g = 2*tau_d`. Try `tau_g = 4*tau_d`.

### T3. Rebalance reward weights

Current: `a_Tn=1, a_Tr=0.25, a_Fn=1, a_Fr=1, a_U=1`.

Try: `a_Tn=2, a_Tr=0, a_Fn=4, a_Fr=0.25, a_U=0.5`. Emphasises NEW (touch/failure) coverage over RARE, removes T_rare (which adds noise).

### T4. Increase UCB exploration coefficient

Current: `c_explore = 0.25`.

Try: `c_explore = 1.0` (and rerun). More exploration → less local optimum.

### T5. Re-derive gamma from budget

Current: `H = max(50, min(300, N/5))` → at N=6000, H=300 → half-life = 300 mutations.

Try: `H = N / 10 = 600` (longer memory, more stable) OR `H = N / 50 = 120` (shorter memory, faster adaptation).

### T6. Lock-in single pilot across all strategies

Currently each strategy may run its own pilot. Lock all 3 to a deterministic shared pilot so tau_new, tau_d, etc. are IDENTICAL across uniform/zoned/bandit. (Removes a confound; doesn't change architecture.)

### T7. Larger budget at N=20000 to test convergence

Run bandit alone at N=20000 with current hyperparameters. If it reaches 43+: bandit needs more budget. If it stays at 37: architecture is broken (proceed to §13).

## 13. Possible improvements (architectural redesign)

These change the architecture more deeply.

### A1. Coverage-greedy reward

Replace the entire reward with:

```
reward = 1.0 if (any new unique local context this mutation) else 0.0
```

This directly targets the goal. The bandit becomes a "discovery rate" maximizer. The drawback is that after ~46 contexts are discovered the reward is always 0 and bandit becomes random.

Variant: reward = `(num_new_contexts_this_run + 1) / (num_failures + 1)` — encourages discovery-per-failure rate.

### A2. Hierarchical structure: kind-level bandit, NOT (kind, bucket)

Reduce arms from 128 to 8. Run UCB at the KIND level only. Step is selected by either uniform-over-valid-steps OR zoned-distribution within kind.

This should converge faster (8 arms = trivial cold-start, 750 pulls each in expectation).

### A3. Diversity-pressured bandit (Thompson sampling)

Replace UCB with Thompson sampling using a Beta-distributed prior. Sample reward distribution per arm; select arm with highest sampled value. This naturally maintains exploration for arms with high reward uncertainty.

### A4. Two-stage bandit: explore-then-exploit

- Stage 1 (first 25% of budget): force uniform-over-kinds sampling (= zoned).
- Stage 2 (remaining 75%): switch to UCB initialised with the empirical rewards from stage 1.

This combines zoned's coverage with bandit's exploitation. Strong if stage 1 is long enough to find rare contexts.

### A5. Per-kind sub-bandit instead of monolithic

Run 8 independent UCB sub-bandits, one per kind. Each selects a step within its kind. At each iteration, sample which kind to use uniformly (or via a meta-bandit that doesn't have Q-penalty issues).

This decouples step-selection optimization (where UCB might help) from kind-selection (where zoned-uniformity wins).

### A6. Reward includes "kind-coverage diversity bonus"

Add a term to the reward that penalises the bandit for over-selecting a kind:

```
reward_adj = reward * exp(-pulls_for_this_kind / target_pulls_per_kind)
```

This is essentially a regularizer toward uniform-kind-distribution, partially recovering zoned's property.

### A7. Decoupled exploration/exploitation: 80/20 mix

At each iteration:
- 80% chance: pick by UCB (exploit)
- 20% chance: pick uniformly over arms (explore)

This is a simple bandit hack but might recover the 3 missed constraints.

Same data, different denominators.

### 17.16 STEP-0 SMOKING GUN: why zoned wins (mechanism revealed)

**The 4 constraints that uniform misses (and that bandit largely misses) are ALL discovered exclusively at step 0, AND only by mutation kind INSTR_TYPE_MOD. The 5th constraint (`ControlUserECALL:77`) is an unrelated MEM_VAL_MOD kind-allocation gap.**

#### 17.16.a — Where (step) each missed constraint is hit, per strategy

|---|---|---|---|

**THIS REFINES THE EARLIER CLAIM**: the 4 zoned-dominated boundary constraints require the **conjunction** `kind = INSTR_TYPE_MOD AND step = 0`. Without BOTH, they do not fire.

#### 17.16.c — INSTR_TYPE_MOD-at-step-0 budget per strategy (the smoking gun count)

Total `INSTR_TYPE_MOD` mutations placed AT step 0, across 5 seeds (30,000 mutations per strategy):

| strategy | ITM @ step=0 | ITM @ step>0 | total ITM | step-0 ITM rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| uniform   | **1**   | 4581 | 4582 | 0.02% |
| zoned     | **184** | 3612 | 3796 | **4.85%** |
| bandit-16 | **2**   | 3017 | 3019 | 0.07% |

So zoned gives the (INSTR_TYPE_MOD, step=0) conjunction ~184 chances per campaign, vs ~1-2 chances for uniform/bandit. Each step-0 INSTR_TYPE_MOD attempt has a ~30-50% chance of firing one of these constraints (depending on the random new (major, minor) draw — must hit MEM0/MEM1/MUL0/DIV0 = 4 of 11 major slots). So zoned's expected hits per campaign = 184 × ~0.4 ≈ 70+ per kind, virtually guaranteeing discovery. Uniform/bandit get ~1 × 0.4 ≈ 0.4 expected hits per kind, hence "MISSED" in most seeds.

Total step=0 mutations per strategy (across all kinds, 5 seeds × 6000 = 30,000):

| strategy | step=0 mutations | step=0 rate | zone weight target |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| uniform   | 4   | 0.013% | (uniform-over-arms ⇒ ~1/B = 1/128 = 0.78% per arm-bucket containing step 0) |
| zoned     | 729 | **2.43%** | (5%-init target, partially diluted because only 3 of 8 kinds have step=0 in their valid_steps list) |
| bandit-16 | 5   | 0.017% | (uniform-over-arms with B=16, same as uniform's per-arm prob ⇒ ~0) |

**Why zoned gets 2.43% and not 5%**: only **3 of the 8 mutation kinds** (`INSTR_TYPE_MOD`, `PRE_EXEC_REG_MOD`, `MEM_VAL_MOD`) include step 0 in their `valid_steps_for_kind` list. The other 5 (`COMP_OUT_MOD`, `LOAD_VAL_MOD`, `STORE_OUT_MOD`, `INSTR_WORD_MOD_FULL`, `INSTR_WORD_MOD_SUR`) require a non-zero step (because step 0 is some specific bootloader-handoff instruction that lacks comparator outputs, load values, store values, etc.). When the fuzzer picks one of those 5 kinds and the zoned selector tries the init zone, the init zone is empty and falls back to core. So the effective step-0 rate ≈ 3/8 × 5% = 1.875%, plus some extra from spillover ≈ 2.43% empirical.

#### 17.16.d — The other "missed" constraint (`ControlUserECALL:77`) is a different mechanism

`ControlUserECALL@inst_control.zir:77` is NOT a step-0 phenomenon. It's hit by `MEM_VAL_MOD` at steps {511..3779}. Bandit hits it 4/5 seeds, uniform hits it 0/5. This is a **kind-allocation gap** (uniform allocates only 8.62% to MEM_VAL_MOD vs zoned's 12.53%, see §17.1), not a step-zone gap.

#### 17.16.e — Architectural implications for ChatGPT Pro

1. **Most of zoned's lead is step-zone coverage** of step 0, not "smarter kind selection".
2. The bandit's reward function is irrelevant to these 4 constraints — they can ONLY be discovered by INSTR_TYPE_MOD@step=0, and bandit's nested step-UCB happens to put ~0 mutations there.
3. **ARCHITECTURAL FIX**: Replace the nested step-UCB with a 5/90/5 zoned step sampler **conditioned on step=0 being valid for the chosen kind**, keep the kind-level UCB. This combines bandit's kind-learning with zoned's mechanical step-zone coverage.
4. **Bonus fix**: Add explicit "boundary cycles" (first, last, and any cycle adjacent to a halt/ECALL/MRET transition) as a separately-weighted zone. The current bootloader-to-user transition at step=0 produces 4+ unique constraints; the user-to-halt transition at step=T-1 may similarly contain hidden constraints we haven't yet probed (zoned reached step T-1 only 2.41% of the time, mostly via COMP_OUT_MOD).

### 17.23 Why is the "universe" only 46 contexts? (zkVMs have hundreds)

**A reader asks**: zkVMs have hundreds of constraints; why are we seeing only 46?

The "universe of 46" is **not a property of the circuit**, it is a property of the **(circuit ∩ mutation-kinds ∩ user-program ∩ trace-instrumentation)** tuple:

1. **Circuit scope**: We instrument the `rv32im` execution circuit only — NOT the recursion circuit, the FRI verifier, the Poseidon2 hashing circuit per se, the SHA accelerator inner state, the BigInt subcircuit, the input-mixing rounds, or the seal-finalisation logic. The rv32im DSL itself defines on the order of 200-400 named constraint sites (one per `assert/eqz` in the `.zir` files). We are saturating only the subset reachable from preflight-trace mutations.

2. **User-program scope**: The single guest binary used in IV.POS.5 is `risc0-host --in1 5 --in4 10`, a minimal SHA-2 test. It exercises:
   - Standard RV32IM ops (ADD, SUB, AND, OR, XOR, SHL, SHR, SLT, AUIPC, JAL, JALR, BEQ/BNE/BLT/BGE, LW/LH/LB/LHU/LBU, SW/SH/SB)
   - MUL/DIV (a few)
   - ECALL/MRET (ecall path for I/O)
   - SHA accelerator (handful of cycles)
   - Memory reads/writes
   It does NOT exercise: BigInt (BIGINT0–7), Poseidon2 user mode, Keccak (separate circuit), custom syscalls (HOST_WRITE/HOST_READ_WORDS variants beyond the basic input read), or unusual control transitions. So the constraints specific to those op families are simply never reached by ANY of our 90,000 mutations.

3. **Mutation-kind scope**: Our 8 kinds touch:
   - `cycles[].major/minor` (INSTR_TYPE_MOD)
   - `cycles[].pc/instruction` (INSTR_WORD_MOD_*)
   - load/store/comparator outputs (LOAD_VAL_MOD, STORE_OUT_MOD, COMP_OUT_MOD)
   - pre-execution register file (PRE_EXEC_REG_MOD)
   - memory residue cells (MEM_VAL_MOD)
   They DO NOT touch: page-fault tables, paging metadata, ECALL argument registers (separately from PRE_EXEC_REG_MOD), accelerator argument buffers (SHA acc registers, BigInt arg slots), session metadata, output digest, or the global checksum lanes. Constraints living in those subsystems cannot be triggered.

4. **Coverage table vs failures table**: The 46 IS the empirical universe; we confirmed that the failures table (all individual failures) contains the exact same 46 distinct `constraint_loc` strings — no additional locs hide there. So 46 is the saturating upper bound for **this** (circuit, program, mutations) tuple, not a partial sample of a larger reachable set.

**What would expand the universe?**

| change | expected universe expansion |
|---|---|
| Add a Poseidon2/BigInt/Keccak-exercising guest program | +20-40 new constraints (one per accelerator op) |
| Mutate within recursion circuit (currently untouched) | +50-100 new constraints |
| Mutate within FRI/poly-commitment (verifier-side) | +30-60 new constraints |
| New mutation kinds touching paging/session metadata | +10-20 |
| Bigger user programs (kernel, real workloads) | +5-15 from rare-but-reachable ops |

So the realistic **all-circuits all-programs** universe is plausibly in the 400-800 range, with our current 46 being the saturating subset of a single sha2-host run with 8 preflight-trace mutation kinds.

**Implication for ChatGPT Pro**: the "46" is not a ceiling we should be embarrassed by — it is a baseline that the architecture has saturated. The next architectural expansion vector is **adding mutation kinds that touch additional substructures** (e.g., paging, accelerator args), or **rotating guest programs** to expand the reachable subset.

The full enumeration of the 46 constraints we currently see is in `§16.2`.

### 17.24 What `b_count`, `num_arms`, and "arms" mean for each strategy

**A reader asks**: why does `uniform` have `b_count=128` and `num_arms=967` while `zoned` has `b_count=NULL` and `num_arms=NULL`? If "arms" is a MAB concept, what does it mean for `uniform` and `zoned`?

**The short answer**: "arms" is the MAB term, but in IV.POS.5 it became a **shared bucketing schema** that two of three strategies (bandit, uniform) draw from. Zoned does not use that schema at all.

#### 17.24.a — What is an "arm"?

An **arm** is a `(mutation_kind, step_bucket)` pair, e.g. `(INSTR_TYPE_MOD, bucket=5)`. Where:
- `mutation_kind ∈ {COMP_OUT_MOD, LOAD_VAL_MOD, ..., INSTR_WORD_MOD_SUR}` (8 values)
- `step_bucket ∈ {0, 1, ..., B_count-1}` (B_count bins partitioning the step axis)

Each arm corresponds to "a region of the (kind, step) space". The bucket maps to a contiguous range of step values via `bucket = step // B`, where `B = ceil(T / B_count)` (`T` is the step horizon ≈ 3930).

#### 17.24.b — How `b_count` is chosen

`b_count` is the number of step buckets. From `arm_universe.py`:

```python
_N_TARGET = 3       # desired average samples per arm
_B_MIN = 16         # minimum step buckets
_B_MAX = 128        # maximum step buckets
raw = budget // (K * _N_TARGET)
B_count = pow2_clamp(raw, _B_MIN, _B_MAX)
```

For budget N=6000 and K=8 kinds: `raw = 6000 // (8 * 3) = 250 → pow2_clamp → 128`.

So **both** bandit and uniform should compute `B_count = 128`. But:

- `bandit-16` explicitly overrides with `b_count_override=16` (chosen during Phase II.2 to keep arm count small enough for UCB to learn meaningfully in 6000 mutations: 8 kinds × 16 buckets = 128 arms with 6000/128 ≈ 47 samples per arm).
- `uniform` does NOT override and uses the formula → `b_count=128`. This gives 8×128 = 1024 candidate arms, but many are empty (some kinds have no valid step in some buckets), leaving **967 available arms**. With 6000 mutations across 967 arms, that's ≈ 6.2 samples per arm — fine for a uniform draw (no learning needed) but too sparse for UCB.
- `zoned` does NOT build an `ArmUniverse` AT ALL — it just calls `selector.select_step(data, kind)` per chosen kind. So `b_count = NULL` and `num_arms = NULL` in the `campaign_params` row (the code writes those columns only if `self.arm_universe is not None`, see `fuzzer.py:_persist_campaign_params`).

#### 17.24.c — Selection algorithm per strategy (clarifying "uniform" vs "zoned")

| strategy | step 1 (pick kind) | step 2 (pick step) | uses ArmUniverse? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `uniform` | jointly with step 2 (see right) | **uniform over (kind, bucket) arms**, then uniform over steps within bucket | **YES** (`UniformArmSelector.select_arm_then_step()`) |
| `zoned` | **uniform over MUTATION_KINDS** | for the chosen kind, uniform over valid_steps_for_kind, weighted by zone: init=5%, core=90%, final=5% | **NO** (`ZonedStepSelector.select_step(data, kind)`) |
| `bandit-16` | (kind, bucket) selected jointly by UCB; step uniform within bucket | UCB-driven over (kind, bucket) arms | **YES** (`DiscountedUCBScheduler`) |

#### 17.24.d — Why "uniform" does NOT mean "uniform-over-trace"

The user's intuition "uniform should sample uniformly over the entire trace, including step 0 and step T-1" is consistent with naming the strategy "uniform". But the actual implementation is:

> **Uniform over the arm universe** = uniform over (kind, bucket) pairs, where each bucket is `B ≈ T/128 ≈ 31` consecutive steps.

The 128 buckets are roughly equal-sized in step count, but:
- Bucket 0 contains steps {0, 1, ..., 30}. Within bucket 0, step 0 has probability 1/31 ≈ 3.2%. So uniform's probability of choosing step 0 within "bucket-0 pulls" is 3.2%, NOT 100%.
- Probability of selecting bucket 0 itself = (number of arms with bucket=0) / 967 ≈ 0.6%.
- So probability of selecting step=0 over all pulls = 0.6% × 3.2% ≈ 0.02%.
- For 6000 mutations: 0.02% × 6000 = **1.2 expected step-0 hits per campaign**. Empirically we observe 4 step-0 mutations across uniform's 30,000 mutations (5 seeds × 6000). Matches.

So "uniform" is uniform-over-arms, which is **practically** core-biased simply because step 0 is one single step among 3930.

For **zoned**, step 0 is its OWN zone, weighted at 5%, so the per-pull step-0 probability is `~5% × (3/8 kinds with step 0 valid) = ~1.9%`, giving 2.43% empirical. That's ~120× more step-0 attempts than uniform per campaign.

**Bottom line**: the names "uniform" and "zoned" reflect WHAT THEY SAMPLE UNIFORMLY OVER, not "uniform over the trace":
- `uniform` ⇒ uniform over (kind, bucket) arms (mirrors bandit's arm space, no learning)
- `zoned` ⇒ uniform over kinds + zoned over steps (5/90/5)
- `bandit` ⇒ UCB over (kind, bucket) arms with discount + reward shaping

#### 17.24.e — Why this metadata difference matters

When ChatGPT Pro reads `campaign_params`, the `(b_count, num_arms)` columns are the ONLY persisted record of the "uniform" arm structure (used by analyze scripts to compute pull histograms). For zoned, these columns are deliberately NULL because the strategy never instantiated an arm structure. This is a **correct schema choice**, not a missing-value bug.

## 18. Budget feasibility: N=10000+ is achievable

Earlier in the campaign (per `POS_PLAYBOOK.md §12.39-§12.41`), we wrongly believed that the 6-hour POS reservation limit capped per-run budget at ~7000 mutations. **This is no longer true.** We discovered during IV.POS.5 (specifically the d3→d4→d5 handoffs) that:

### 18.1 The "continuous reservation" pattern

POS calendar reservations can be created back-to-back for the same nodes. Per `POS_PLAYBOOK.md §12.40`: **allocations persist past their calendar event end_date if a follow-up reservation for the same nodes/owner immediately succeeds**. Combined with the 2-future-entry cap (`§12.33`), the workflow is:

```
At reservation N start (say 03:00 CEST):
  - allocation grants nodes
  - dispatch starts
At reservation N+1 start (09:00 CEST), if N+1 was pre-booked at 03:30:
  - allocation TRANSFERS automatically (no kill, no relaunch)
  - dispatch keeps running uninterrupted
```

This means a single run can span MULTIPLE 6-hour reservation windows seamlessly.

### 18.2 N=10000 wall-clock estimate

| N | wall time per run | reservation windows needed | feasible? |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 6,000 | 4.8h | 1 | ✅ DONE |
| 10,000 | 8.0h | 2 (with continuity) | ✅ confirmed feasible |
| 20,000 | 16.0h | 3 (with continuity) | ✅ feasible |
| 50,000 | 40.0h | 7 (with continuity, manage cap) | ⚠️ doable but tedious; needs auto-renew script |

### 18.3 Parallelism constraint

We run 3 nodes in parallel (`flare`, `octorand`, `opulous`), so a 3-strategy dispatch fits in one reservation window. For N=10000:
- Per dispatch (1 seed × 3 strategies): ~8h
- 5 dispatches (5 seeds): ~40h sequential = ~7 reservation windows = ~2 calendar days
- TOTAL: ~2 days for a complete N=10000 A/B campaign (vs ~1 day for N=6000)

For N=20000, ~4 calendar days total.

### 18.4 Practical implication for ChatGPT Pro

**We are NOT budget-constrained at N≤20000.** If the answer to "would the MAB converge given more budget?" requires testing at N=20000, we can do it within a week. Pro should feel free to recommend N=10000 or N=20000 follow-ups without worrying about feasibility.

Higher Ns (N=50000+) are doable but require:
- A "renew calendar entry" cron-style script (we have the building blocks in `auto_run_ab_v1_smart.sh`).
- More careful management of the 2-future-entry cap.

| Kind | Targets |
|---|---|

| CYCLE_DIFF_COUNT_MOD | `cycles[].diff_count` |

| CRYPTO_STATE_MOD | `backs[]` (Poseidon2State, Sha2State) |
| ECALL_BACK_MOD | `backs[]` (Ecall variant) |
| STRUCTURAL_MOD | `table_split_cycle`, `rand_z` |

### 19.5 Why this matters for the MAB architecture

The taxonomy doc identifies **23 distinct (variable, context) pairs** that are mutable. We currently exercise 8 of them (35%). Adding the high-priority pair (TXN_PREV_WORD_MOD + TXN_PREV_CYCLE_MOD) would expand the arm space from 8 → 10 kinds × 16 buckets = 160 arms (vs current 128). This could:

- **Expand coverage** if these mutations hit currently-unreachable constraints (likely — they're the only mutations targeting `prev_word` and `prev_cycle`, two of the 5 fields in RawMemoryTransaction).
- **Increase per-kind data sparsity** (each kind gets fewer samples), making bandit convergence harder.
- **Potentially shift the MAB's pathology** if some new kinds have high discovery rate per mutation (like INSTR_TYPE_MOD does today).

ChatGPT Pro should consider:
- Whether expanding the kind space is **complementary** or **substitutable** to fixing the reward function.
- Whether the architecture's reward signal would correctly value the new kinds, or repeat the INSTR_WORD_MOD_SUR pathology with whichever new kind has the highest Q_loc.

### 19.6 Strategic question

**If the current MAB cannot reliably exceed zoned at N=6000 with 8 kinds, will it do so at N=10000 with 10 kinds?** Two competing hypotheses:

- **Pro-expansion**: more kinds = more discovery potential = MAB has more material to work with.
- **Anti-expansion**: more kinds = sparser data per arm = MAB's learning is slower, and the reward-misranking pathology has more arms to play out on.

Pro should weigh in on this.
